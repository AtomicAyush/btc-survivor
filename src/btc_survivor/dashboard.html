How to read this page

Runs. Each tab is a separate experiment. A has retail fees and $10,000 per bot, B has a 5 bps fee and $10,000, and C has retail fees and $500 so deaths come within weeks. All three start from identical bots.

Learners and twins. Each run has 20 small learning bots. Each one has a coin flip twin that trades at the same moments but picks long or short by coin toss, so comparing the two separates skill from luck.

Money. Every bot starts with paper money, pays real fees on a fixed $500 trade and a little rent each healthy minute, and dies when it cannot afford one more round trip and a day of rent. A dead bot is replaced in the same minute.

Feed. Bots only act in healthy minutes, when the Coinbase data is connected, fresh and consistent. Recovering or stale means everything is paused.

Stage. A launch collects 6 hours of history, waits for 30 minute outcomes to mature so predictions can be graded, trains every bot through a warmup, and only then lets them trade.

Forced trades. At these fees a bot's prediction almost never clears the cost of trading, so most trades are forced trades made every 12 hours to keep the bots gathering experience.

Skill. The headline result. It is the correlation between the bots' average prediction and what Bitcoin actually did, scored once per 30 minutes after the first day, with a plain linear model beside it. It is only final on day 28.

Benchmarks. Buy and hold put all the money into Bitcoin at launch. Matched bought only $500 of it. Cash is start money minus rent, the bot that never trades.

Freshness. The page refreshes every 10 seconds. Bot rows come from a save every 5 minutes, and all times are UTC.
